## 2. Corrections to the research set — read these before sizing anything

**2.1 The PDT rule no longer exists, and one of your sources built the whole plan on it.**
The SEC approved removing the $25,000 minimum on 2026-04-14; FINRA made it effective
**2026-06-04**. The $25k floor, the four-trades-in-five-days counter and the 90-day
freeze are gone, replaced by risk-based intraday margin. So the T+1 cash-account
model — fixed $4,000 partitions, risk cut to 0.20–0.50% — is solving a constraint
that was removed three months ago. **Do not adopt it.** Size on risk.
*Caveat:* broker-dealers have an 18-month phase-in and house requirements still
vary. Confirm with your broker before assuming the new framework is live on your
account.

**2.2 EQPT is not a semiconductor equipment company.** It is **EquipmentShare.com**
— construction equipment rental. It IPO'd 2026-01-23 at $24.50 and trades around
$18.56 (roughly −24% from IPO). It appears to have entered the screen on a name
collision with "equipment." It is also disqualified on history (~155 sessions, no
valid SMA200).

**2.3 CRDO is Credo Technology Group**, not "Croda International" — one source got
this wrong. Credo's Q1 FY27 (reported 9/1) was a genuine beat: EPS $1.20 vs $1.12,
revenue $479M **+115% YoY**, Q2 guide $525–535M. But the +8.82% to $282.82 print
circulating in the research is dated **"Monday" (pre-earnings positioning)**, not
9/4. Recompute the post-earnings path from your own bars before trading it.

**2.4 Your account size is inconsistent across your own sources.** One thread says
$20,000 at 1% ($200/trade); two say $25,000 at 1.5% ($375/trade). Pick one — the
sizing code is parameterized, but the plan cannot be.

**2.5 ADBE has not reported yet.** Earnings are **2026-09-10**, with options pricing
a ±6.99% (~$19.96) move. It is a forward binary, not a 9/4 result, and it does not

**2.6 ADSK's +9.1% could not be dated.** It may be the FY2026 Q2 print from August
2025 rather than anything current. Treated as disqualified until confirmed.

## 4. Why consensus scoring is capped

You asked for tickers weighted up when they recur across the AI chat filters. That
is implemented — but with diminishing returns and a hard cap at 3.0, because those
chats are **not independent observers**. They read the same wire feeds and the same
mover tables. Five mentions is one observation echoed five times, not five samples.

So the score decides **ranking only**. It can never admit a trade. Admission belongs
entirely to the mechanical gate against real bars, which holds a veto. `GOLD` ranks
#1 on consensus *and* carries `extended_group` — its sector ran ~35% in six weeks
into an +11% day. Ranked first, and still has to clear the gate like everything else.

Also worth stating plainly: only **3 of the 6** declared chat sources actually had
transcripts (Grok, Claude, Gemini). ChatGPT, Copilot and Zoom rows are declared but
empty. Scores are computed over three sources, not six. Add their tickers to the
`mentions` arrays in the JSON and re-run — nothing else changes.

## 5. The deterministic rule set

Every rule is a computable condition. No discretion at the point of execution.

### Gate 1 — Regime (evaluate once, 09:45 ET, then stop re-evaluating)

Compare SPY to its prior close, and SMH's opening-15-minute return to SPY's:

| SPY vs prior close | SMH vs SPY | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Up | Outperforming | Full risk — up to **3** positions |
| **Down** | **Outperforming** | **Half risk — max 2 positions, longs only inside the leading cluster** |
| Up | Lagging | 1 position, in whichever cluster actually leads |
| Down | Lagging | **No longs. Cash.** |

Friday was row 2. If Tuesday repeats it, you are in the half-risk, relative-strength
regime — not the full-risk one.

**Energy overlay, added for this week.** Check the front-month crude print at 09:45
alongside the table above:

- **Crude up more than 3% from Friday's settle** → the inflation leg is driving.
  Drop one position slot from whatever the table allows (so row 1 permits 2, row 2
  permits 1) and **do not open a position in `enterprise_software`** — the longest-
  duration cluster on the list is the first thing sold when the CPI path repriced.
- **Crude flat or lower** → the weekend escalation is being faded. Run the table as
  written.

This overlay only ever *removes* risk. It can never add a slot the base table did
not already grant.

### Gate 2 — Mechanical admission (run pre-market, per name)

`mechanical_filters.screen()`. Every check must pass; **any unknown is a rejection**:

- ≥200 sessions of history (no valid SMA200 → no trade; this auto-kills CBRS and EQPT)
- Close > SMA20 > SMA50 > SMA200
- 20-day average dollar volume ≥ $25M
- ATR(14) between 1.5% and 12% of price
- Close ≤ 3.0 ATR above SMA20 (extension cap)
- Bid-ask ≤ 0.30% of mid **at the moment of entry**
- Price ≥ $5.00

### Gate 3 — Entry trigger (opening-range continuation)

Opening range = 09:30–09:45 ET high/low. Go long **only** when all five hold:

1. Passed Gate 2 pre-market
2. Open gap ≤ **+4%** vs Friday's close — above that the move is already gone
3. Price breaks and holds above the OR high
4. Price above VWAP
5. Breakout bar volume > 1.5× the average of the opening-range bars

**No entries after 11:30 ET. Never add to a loser. Never buy at the open** — after a
three-day weekend the opening print is the single worst fill of the session.

### Gate 4 — Stop placement (before sizing, always)

Structural stop = **opening-range low − $0.05**. Then validate the distance:

- Distance > 1.5 × ATR(14) daily → **skip the trade** (too wide to size sanely)
- Distance < 0.4 × ATR(14) daily → **skip the trade** (noise will take you out)

The stop defines the trade. If the stop does not work, there is no trade — you do
not widen it to make the position fit.

### Gate 5 — Sizing

```
shares = (equity × risk%) ÷ (entry − stop),  capped at 25% of equity in notional
```

```bash
python scripts/build_watchlist.py --size 25000 1.5 84.30 82.10
```

The 25% notional cap is what makes risk-based sizing safe: a very tight stop implies
an enormous share count, and a **gap through** that stop loses far more than one R.
When the cap binds, actual risk is *below* one R — the tool reports that explicitly
rather than letting you overstate expectancy.

### Gate 6 — Exits

- Scale **50% at +1R**; move the stop on the remainder to breakeven
- Trail the remainder below the 9-EMA on the 5-minute chart
- **Hard time stop: flat by 15:50 ET.** Intraday horizon means intraday.
- **Two losses in a session → stop trading for the day.** Daily loss limit 2R.

## 8. What would invalidate this plan

- **SPY and SMH both open weak** → row 4 of Gate 1. No longs. This plan does nothing
  that day, and that is the plan working.
- **The semis complex gaps up more than 4% across the board on 9/8** → every name

  without you, which is better than buying its end.
- **Another beat-and-raise gets sold** (watch ADBE on 9/10) → the good-news-sold
  pattern is confirmed and the long side of this playbook should be stood down until
  it stops.
- **Yields keep rising on hike expectations.** High-multiple growth is the first

## 9. Honest limits of this document

Every price, percentage and date in Sections 1–2 is sourced and dated. **No SMA20 /
SMA50 / SMA200 value, average dollar volume, or spread appears anywhere in this
document**, because market-data hosts are unreachable from the environment that
built it and inventing those numbers would be worse than omitting them. Those are
computed at runtime by `mechanical_filters.py` against your own Alpaca/yfinance
bars, and the gate **fails closed** when it cannot get them. That is deliberate:
a rejection you have to override is safe; a fabricated pass is not.

Nothing here is investment advice, and the code places no orders — `engine.py` is
hardcoded to Alpaca's paper endpoint and the 60-day trial gate still applies.
